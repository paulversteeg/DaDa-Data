# Information architecture

The structure of the show: its parts, their order and how they connect. For a show, the "screens" are the sections and the "navigation" is the order in which the audience moves through them. Source: the show description "Dada Ex Machina" (JIP '27 omschrijving) and the planning sheet. Total show length: about 4:45 to 5:45 minutes (the A Class limit is 3 to 6 minutes).

## Screen list

The show has four sections. The first two are connected and are treated as one part, so the planning sheet's "deel 1 / 2 / 3" runs like this:

| Planning part | Section | Duration | Sound | Members |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deel 1 | The Algorithmic Prison (the soulless intro) | 1:00-1:15 | Electronic | Uniform, all the same |
| Deel 1 | The Scream (the Dada rebellion) | 1:15-1:30 | Electronic and acoustic | Uniform versus individual |
| Deel 2 | The Wonder (the rest point) | 0:30-0:45 | Acoustic | Individual |
| Deel 3 | The Human Showcase (the party) | 2:00-2:15 | Acoustic | Individual |

## Navigation structure

The audience moves through the sections in a fixed order, from machine to human:

1. The Algorithmic Prison (cold, electronic, uniform)
2. The Scream (the break, uniform versus individual)
3. The Wonder (the rest point, quiet and acoustic)
4. The Human Showcase (the party, acoustic and individual)

The first two sections are one part (deel 1), so the show moves through three parts.

## The sections

**The Algorithmic Prison**

- **Goal:** put the concept down directly and clearly.
- **Pacing:** not too long. The cold, emotionless and perfect machine starts tight and metronome-like, as if the members were an algorithm running a script. Once the jury and audience understand the machine, it is time to break the system.
- **Look:** camouflaged by masks or helmets, black-and-white polka dots.
- **Movement:** tutting, robotic isolations and extremely tight, symmetrical drill lines.

**The Scream**

- **Goal:** build tension, show contrast and fight out the conflict of human against machine.
- **Story:** one member makes a mistake, an expression of emotion. The AI system (the rest of the ensemble) detects it as an anomaly and tries to suppress it. The member fights back and slowly "infects" the others until the system gives in to humanity.
- **Moments:** the glitch (tight walking movements stutter and stall) and the rebellion (masks come off one by one, as a chain reaction).
- **Drill:** "the Swarm", chaotic but controlled running in all directions instead of marching.
- **Dance:** a shift to heavy, modern dance (contemporary release) and the "Pythago-Cubist" triangle hand gesture.
- **Pacing:** the tempo can fluctuate. The electronics drop out halfway with a "pop", then the ensemble speeds up (accelerando), uses vocal screams and hits raw metal percussion for a purely acoustic, wild closing chord.

**The Wonder**

- **Context:** the AI system has just crashed. The loud rhythmic chaos stops abruptly.
- **Goal:** a moment of pure vulnerability. The members realise they are free of the algorithm and are suddenly human again. Without it telling them what to do, they have to rediscover how to feel, breathe and cooperate.
- **Music:** based on "Enjoy the Silence", arranged by Tijn (see `features/muziek-deel-2/`).
- **Sound:** it starts in complete silence. One member begins a soft, uncertain acoustic motif with bare hands on the drum rim, and a member on the other side of the room answers. The drummers put down their sticks.
- **Drill:** the tight blocks dissolve into an asymmetric, scattered, crouching setup.
- **Movement:** visible, theatrical breathing and looking at each other's hands and faces in wonder.

**The Human Showcase**

- **Goal:** celebrate the victory, show endurance and bring the house down. This is the longest and most energetic section, and the members spend the physical energy they saved in the first half.
- **Sound:** pure, driving rock groove, ending in a gigantic consonant climax that celebrates human creativity.
- **Drill:** wide, flowing and open.
- **Showmanship:** stick tricks, challenging eye contact and individual freedom in poses.
- **Ending:** no tight hero pose. The members show theatrical gasping, sweating and physical exhaustion as the ultimate proof of humanity.

## Superseded parts of the show description

The show description has been overtaken on these points (see `platform/history/dont-do.md`):

- **Props:** the moving "AI robot" props (members blocking them, taking manual control, using them as toys) are out. There are no props on the field this season. The only exception is the floor, a large plastic mat.
- **Smoke:** the smoke in the description is not part of the show.
- **Dance:** there is no dance while carrying the drums this season, and whether there will be real dance at all is still to be seen. The dance elements in the description (contemporary release in The Scream) are therefore not confirmed. What is planned is simple movement, described below.

## Movement

The movement is simple. It is not a dance routine.

- **Hard, tight movements:** there are about five sharp movements that every member masters, with the head, arms or a leg. Individual members perform them in sequence, for example in the opening, where the show is more robotic and staccato. They may be taken over from one member to the next, and it must look like a computer. Everything must be tight.
- **Movement while playing:** possible in the opening and at other places, and simpler still.
- **The second part (the quiet part):** more reflection and appreciation of the artistic and the round, so many more round, flowing movements.

## Layers

- **Junior layer:** the playful, absurd visual style. Repeating rhythmic blocks (the "mantras") and simple, tight geometric figures in the drill. Visually funny and very tangible theatre.
- **Senior layer:** the friction and psychological tension of the music. Embracing the uncomfortable and breaking traditions. They carry the more complex rhythmic layers and the "outsider" rock attitude.
- **Jury and audience:** the jury appreciates the clear, Dada design. The audience enjoys the raw, hypnotic live energy.

## Where new features usually land

Not applicable to a show with a fixed order. A new element (a moment, a movement or a sound) lands in the section whose goal it serves.

## Soundscape

The soundscape is the layer underneath the whole show. It runs through all four sections. Details and decisions are in `features/soundscape/`. In the planning sheet it is made by Paul, with samples for deel 2 and deel 3 listed as separate items. The sound rules for electronics and samples are in `platform/tech/tech-constraints.md`.
